The one user + pain

A newly-married couple in their first year of combined finances — two incomes, two old habits, no shared system yet. Today they keep a spreadsheet that goes stale within weeks, and overspend without noticing.

Data model + seed data

Resource: transactions (renamed from the template's items) — date, description, amount (REAL, negative = expense, positive = income), category.

Seed data: ~20 hand-written transactions for one realistic couple-month — rent, groceries (×4), streaming subscriptions (×3), dining out (×3), gas, utilities, gym, coffee runs. Income ≈ $6,200. Real names and amounts, never "lorem ipsum".

White space: no app is free, zero-setup, couple-native, and able to answer "can we afford this?" in plain English.

  • ✅ Search demand — FOUND
    "YNAB alternatives 2026" and "Mint alternatives 2026" are high-volume; Mint shut down in 2024, leaving millions hunting for a replacement. Sources: NerdWallet, Envelope Budgeting.
  • ✅ People complaining — FOUND
    Top reasons users leave YNAB: $109/yr price, steep learning curve, sync issues. Source: NerdWallet review roundup.
  • ✅ Someone already making money — FOUND
    YNAB ($109/yr) and Monarch Money (~$99/yr) prove willingness to pay. Source: Pine AI.

TAM/SAM/SOM: TAM ~2.7M Canadian couples × $20/yr affiliate value ≈ $54M CAD/yr. SAM ~400k actively switching × $20/yr ≈ $8M CAD/yr. SOM (next 12 mo) 1,000–5,000 via Demo Night + referral ≈ $20k–$100k CAD.

The wedge: "The budgeting app for new couples that answers 'can we afford this?' instead of just showing you charts." Why now: Mint's 2024 shutdown freed millions of users, AI chat is the expected UX, and cost-of-living pressure makes shared control urgent.
