Stage 3 — Opportunity

Is it worth building — and what's the edge?

You know the one screen now. Size the market, find the gap, and get a story you can pitch on Demo Night.

Inputs · what you told the AI

  • Who or what do people use instead today?
    Recipe blogs (SEO-bloated, 12 ingredients), big recipe apps (Mealime, Yummly — built for planned shops), and just Googling "what can I make with…".
  • What would make your user switch?
    It starts from "what's already in my fridge" — 3 ingredients in, one cookable recipe out, in under 20 minutes. No shop, no scroll.
  • How big could this get?
    Start: one campus. Then every post-secondary student in Canada who cooks for themselves on a budget.
  • Would anyone pay?
    Maybe a few dollars/month for premium (save pantry, meal history), but the demo is free — paid meal apps prove people already pay for this.

The white space: nobody starts from "what's already in my fridge" for a broke student. That column is the whole positioning.

Three demand signals

  • Search demand — found. "what can I make with [ingredients]" is a high-volume, evergreen search; recipe-by-ingredient is a known query pattern.
  • People complaining — found. Reddit threads (r/EatCheapAndHealthy, r/Cooking) on bloated blog recipes, decision fatigue, and student food waste.
  • Someone already making money — found. Mealime, Yummly, SuperCook and paid meal-planners exist — proof people pay to solve a version of this.

All three signals present → the market is real. The job is the wedge, not proving demand.

Back-of-napkin sizing (assumptions visible)

  • TAM — ~2M post-secondary students in Canada × ~$50/yr (if they'd pay) = ~$100M.
  • SAM — ~600k who regularly cook for themselves on a budget × ~$50/yr = ~$30M.
  • SOM (next 1–2 yrs) — 3 campuses, ~5k users, a slice paying = ~$100k.

The number isn't the point — the bottom-up thinking is what a Demo Night judge will ask about.

The wedge

"The recipe app for broke students that only uses what's already in your fridge."

Why you: you are the user — a student who's stared into that half-empty fridge. Why now: grocery prices are up, students are cooking more, and AI makes ingredient-matching cheap and instant. Everyone else optimises the planned shop; FridgeChef owns the "right now, what I have" moment.
