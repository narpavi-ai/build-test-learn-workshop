Stage 1 — Problem

Who hurts, and why?

One user, one pain, in a single sentence — before any tool.

Inputs · what you told the AI

  • In one sentence, what's the idea?
    "FridgeChef — you type the few ingredients you actually have and it tells you something you can cook tonight."
  • Who exactly feels this pain?
    A broke, busy student in a shared apartment with a half-empty fridge and ~20 minutes before they give up and order in.
  • What do they do today instead?
    Stare into the fridge, Google "what can I make with eggs and…", get overwhelmed by 12-ingredient blog recipes, then order takeout.
  • Why does that hurt?
    Money (takeout adds up fast) and waste (food goes off unused) — plus the small daily stress of "what's for dinner".
  • Why now?
    Grocery prices are up and students are cooking more, but recipe sites are built for planned shops, not "what's in my fridge right now".

Outputs · what the AI gave back

Problem statement

Broke, busy students struggle to turn the random ingredients they already have into dinner because recipe sites assume a planned grocery shop. Today they Google and give up, which costs them money on takeout and wasted food.

Supporting points

  • The person: lives in a share house, cooks for one, low budget, low patience.
  • The moment: 7pm, hungry, fridge open, decision fatigue.
  • "Solved" feels like: type 3 ingredients → get one recipe I can start in 2 minutes.

FridgeChef's whole reason to exist: turn "random ingredients" into "dinner, sorted."
