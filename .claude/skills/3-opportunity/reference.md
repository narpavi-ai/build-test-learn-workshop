# Stage 3 — Opportunity · founder guidance

This is the depth behind the SKILL.md beats. Read it before doing the work — it
has the frameworks, the *why* (so you can handle ideas the templates don't cover),
the worked Kora example, and where to look online.

## Why this stage exists (and why it's after Spec)

Founders fall in love with a build and never check whether anyone else solved it,
how many people have the pain, or why a user would switch. This stage is the
reality check — not to kill the idea, but to give the founder an honest map and a
sharp story. We run it **after** the spec because once you know the exact one
screen you're building, the competitor read and the sizing are about a *real*
thing, and the wedge you find feeds straight into the build and the Demo Night
pitch.

Tone: encouraging and honest. "No one else does this" usually means "no market" —
competition is a *good* sign. The job is to find the **gap**, not to win every box.

## 1. Competitor white-space grid

List 3–5 real alternatives — and always include the **current workaround** from
the problem stage (Googling, a spreadsheet, giving up); that's the true
competitor. Score each on 3–5 dimensions the *one user* actually cares about.

```
                | Speed | Made for | Free | Works with   | <your edge>
                |       | students |      | what I have  |
----------------|-------|----------|------|--------------|------------
Recipe blogs    |  ✗    |   ✗      |  ✓   |     ✗        |    ✗
Big recipe apps |  ~    |   ✗      |  ~   |     ✗        |    ✗
Just Googling   |  ✗    |   ✗      |  ✓   |     ✗        |    ✗
YOU (the spec)  |  ✓    |   ✓      |  ✓   |     ✓        |    ✓
```

The **white space** is the column/cell where everyone else is ✗ and you're ✓.
That gap is your positioning. If you're ✓ everywhere and they're ✗ everywhere, be
suspicious — you've probably understated the competition.

## 2. The three demand signals

A market exists when these align. Search for each and report honestly (found / not
found / mixed) with a source link:
- **Search demand** — people actively looking. Look at Google autocomplete/Trends,
  App Store search, YouTube "how to" volume.
- **People complaining** — search Reddit, app-store reviews, forums for frustration
  with today's options. Real complaints = real pain.
- **Someone already making money** — an existing paid product/creator/service
  doing a version of this. Proof the pain is worth paying to solve.

All three missing is a yellow flag (maybe no market). All three present is green —
your job is just the wedge.

## 3. Back-of-napkin TAM / SAM / SOM

Keep it bottom-up and student-sized — assumptions visible, not a McKinsey deck.
- **TAM** (whole pie): everyone with the pain × yearly value.
  e.g. ~2M post-secondary students in Canada × $50/yr if they'd pay = **$100M**.
- **SAM** (who you could realistically serve): the slice your product+go-to-market
  reaches. e.g. English-speaking students who cook for themselves ≈ 600k × $50 = **$30M**.
- **SOM** (next 1–2 years): who you could actually get. e.g. 3 campuses, 5k users,
  some paying ≈ **$50–150k**.
Show the multiplication. The number isn't the point — the *thinking* is, and it's
exactly what an investor or judge will ask on Demo Night.

Common misuse: top-down "1% of a $10B market". Don't. Build up from your one user.

## 4. The wedge

One tight paragraph + a one-liner:
- **Why you** — the unfair insight/access you have (you *are* the user, a community, a skill).
- **Why now** — what changed that makes this solvable/urgent today.
- **One-liner positioning:** "the [category] for [one user] that [the edge]."
  e.g. *"the budgeting app for new couples that answers 'can we afford this?' instead of just showing charts."*

If the research sharpened the problem (a tighter user, a clearer edge), say so and
note what `/4a-lovable` or `/4b-scope-design` should carry forward.

## Where to look (fast, student-friendly)

- **Reddit** — `site:reddit.com <pain>` surfaces real complaints and workarounds.
- **App Store / Play Store** — search the category; read the 1–3 star reviews.
- **Product Hunt / Google** — who's already shipping near this.
- **Google Trends / autocomplete** — is interest rising, flat, seasonal?
Keep it to a few searches — this is a sanity check, not a thesis.

## Worked example — Kora

- **Grid gap:** Mint/YNAB/Monarch show you charts of what happened; none answer "can we afford this?" in plain English, free, with zero setup, built for two people. That cell is the white space.
- **Signals:** search — "Mint/YNAB alternatives 2026" is high-volume (Mint shut down in 2024); complaints — YNAB's price and learning curve drive users away; money — YNAB ($109/yr) and Monarch (~$99/yr) prove willingness to pay.
- **Napkin sizing:** TAM ~2.7M CA couples × $20/yr = ~$54M; SAM ~400k actively switching × $20 = ~$8M; SOM 1–5k couples via referral = ~$20k–100k.
- **Wedge:** *"The budgeting app for new couples that answers 'can we afford this?' instead of just showing charts."* Why now: Mint's shutdown freed millions, AI chat is the expected UX, cost-of-living makes control urgent.
